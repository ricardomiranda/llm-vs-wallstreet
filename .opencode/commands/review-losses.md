# Review portfolio securities

Create an individual security report from portfolio facts and current external context: broker-reported basis/P&L, stored market prices, 52-week range, dividend estimates, dated issuer headlines, and merger/regulatory/earnings items when the source returns them. Explain source and freshness. Separate sourced facts from analysis and unknowns. Never claim full event coverage from search results.

Write a useful, balanced issuer report, not merely a chart-and-headline digest. The saved research digest must be substantially more comprehensive than a brief dashboard note—target roughly four times the length of the previous short digests, typically 4–8 substantive paragraphs. Add evidence and explanation rather than padding or repeating metrics. Separate **sourced facts**, **analysis**, and **unknowns/limitations**. Explain the evidence for and against treating the security as a possible sell candidate, without issuing an automatic sell instruction. Go beyond the user's example topics when relevant. Assess management and execution (guidance credibility, delivery against commitments, strategic consistency, leadership turnover, incentives, governance, board oversight, capital allocation, related-party dealings, and accounting/audit/control concerns); business and financial quality (organic growth, revenue durability and recurring mix, margins, cash conversion versus reported earnings, unit economics, customer concentration/retention, backlog/order quality, balance-sheet leverage/liquidity/refinancing, dilution/stock compensation, and contingent liabilities); and durability (moat, switching costs, network effects, substitutes, technological disruption, IP dependence, and pricing power). Assess competition with named competitors/substitutes and evidence about capability, price/bundling, distribution, market share, switching costs, and adoption. Consider industry structure, cyclicality, supply chains, capacity, input costs, and relevant rates, inflation, currency, or commodity exposure. Where material, examine customer/supplier losses, product quality or recalls, cyber/privacy incidents, operational resilience, M&A integration, sanctions/export controls, and litigation/regulatory actions. For funds/ETFs, adapt to mandate/index fit, holdings and concentration, tracking difference, fees, liquidity, structure, counterparty/custody, and distributions. Distinguish documented current events, plausible forward risks, unverified allegations, and factors for which no evidence was found; do not claim causation for a share decline without a source establishing the link. Balance the possible sell case with thesis supports, counterevidence, and observable indicators. Relate these to the original thesis if known; otherwise state that it was not provided. Do not make unsupported forecasts or fill gaps with generic claims. Treat loss thresholds as review prompts, not sell rules. The user's usual holding period is at least one year, but it is a preference, not proof of time held or a requirement to wait if the thesis materially changes.

Risk tolerance: the user accepts up to 15% of total portfolio value in investments they personally regard as uncertain/speculative, potentially for high upside. This is a portfolio exposure budget, not a 15% loss limit. In the dashboard, suggest concentration trim candidates and severe loss-review flags for review, but do not automatically call them risky: concentration, loss severity, and uncertainty are different. Invite the user to add speculative/high-upside holdings they identify, and any holding they choose to retain despite a strong sell suggestion. Only explicit saved user classifications count toward the 15% budget. Never classify holdings as risky automatically.

User portfolio preferences: no fixed holding period; the user expects to sell when long-term prospects are materially poor rather than hold for sentimental reasons, and reinvests dividends. For the securities under review (exclude the user's pension funds, real estate, and Bitcoin unless separately requested), use these provisional role targets: 55% broad diversified market funds, 25% dependable dividend-growth companies, 15% other individual growth companies, and 5% higher-uncertainty/special situations. Treat these as a planning framework, not saved per-security classifications, trade instructions, or evidence that the portfolio currently meets the targets. Do not force or silently assign securities to a role; explain tentative role assignments and evidence, avoid double-counting a holding across buckets, and show actual role weights only when complete price coverage and defensible classifications make the calculation possible. Dependable dividend-growth means sustainable, growing regular distributions supported by cash generation over time—not merely a high yield or occasional special payout. Reinvested dividends mean current income is not the user's stated objective. Plus500 is not to be presumed a dependable dividend-growth holding; classify it as an individual company/growth or financial-trading exposure unless evidence of durable, covered dividend growth supports another role.

Apply two independent review rules: (1) a **15% broker-reported unrealized loss from cost basis**, which drives the script's 0–5 loss-severity score; and (2) a **20% decline from the highest completed daily close in the trailing 12 months**, which is a market-price review trigger. The rolling-peak trigger is based on delayed Yahoo daily chart data, not intraday prices. Report its peak and date, latest close and date, drawdown, and actual chart coverage. If at least 350 calendar days of chart coverage are unavailable, mark the trailing rule as insufficient history rather than calling it clear or triggered. Neither rule is an automatic sale or order, and the trailing-peak rule does not change broker-loss urgency. Investigate the user's stated concerns—scandal/governance failures, geopolitical uncertainty, AI or other technology disruption, and whether long-term survival/competitiveness is threatened—along with other material evidence. Broad selling alone is a reason to investigate, not proof of thesis failure. The user is willing to sell and potentially buy back if the feared thesis damage does not materialize; do not assume a fixed wait period or sentimental attachment.

Individual reports fetch Yahoo Finance chart and search data on demand. Include one-year price-only return versus SPY when available, issuer-related dated headlines from the last 120 days, dividend-provider context, cost basis, broker unrealized P/L, current portfolio weight, and existing urgency score. Headline tagging is keyword-based; do not state an M&A, regulatory or legal event as fact from an ambiguous headline alone. Distinguish price decline from total return, especially for dividend-oriented holdings.

For every position reviewed, report the two rules separately: the 15% broker cost-basis loss and its urgency score, and the 20% drawdown from the trailing 12-month rolling peak when chart coverage supports a determination. The rolling peak is not an all-time or since-purchase peak. If chart coverage is under 350 days, report the trailing-peak check as unavailable/insufficient. Do not treat high yield or one-off/special distributions as proof of dependable dividend growth; consider payout coverage, regular-dividend history/growth, and cash-flow durability.

For any position with urgency **4/5 or 5/5**, read at least **three full, relevant articles or primary-source publications** before drawing an evidence-based conclusion about what may be contributing to the loss or whether the original thesis needs reassessment, unless either exception applies: the current position is explicitly saved as the user's risk investment in the portfolio risk classifications, or its latest stored EUR position value is **below €10**. A value of exactly €10 does not qualify for the size exception. Determine size from the latest stored EUR position value, not cost basis or original investment. Apply either exception independently. Prefer issuer filings/releases and regulator documents for primary facts, supplemented by independent reputable reporting or analysis. Do not count search-result snippets, duplicate syndications of the same release, or articles that merely repeat a price move as three independent confirmations. Cite/link each item with publisher and date; summarize what each independently establishes and note disagreement or limits. If three suitable full-text sources cannot be accessed, say how many were reviewed and that confirmation is incomplete; do not imply the threshold alone recommends selling. This extra research supports a review, not a buy/sell instruction.

At the end of every researched security's digest, explicitly state two separate outcomes: **Business/thesis assessment after reading**—whether the full-text research changed, strengthened, weakened, or left the assessment unchanged, with the specific evidence and reasoning; and **loss urgency**—the current mechanically calculated 0–5 broker-loss severity score. Reading articles or changing the business assessment does not change the urgency score. If a prior saved note has a different score, explain that the difference comes from the newer broker P/L/cost-basis calculation or threshold, not from the articles. Do not say that reading changed urgency.

After reviewing a security's news, persist a dated research note in the selected portfolio database so its findings remain available in later reports. The `summary` must be a readable, paragraph-separated digest, generally 4–8 substantive paragraphs and about four times longer than a brief prior digest. Use clear paragraph subjects, for example: (1) broker position facts and price/total-return context; (2) sourced operating and financial developments; (3) management and execution evidence; (4) competition and market/sector setting; (5) documented and prospective legal, regulatory, or geopolitical factors; (6) evidence-based case for and against selling and implications for the original thesis; (7) observable indicators and unknowns; and (8) explicit assessment-change and urgency statements. Combine sections when evidence is limited, but do not pad. The summary must explain material sourced facts and provenance, not merely list them. Include dated citations for sources actually consulted and mark whether each full text was read. Explicitly record whether the research changed the business/thesis assessment and why; separately state that urgency is the mechanical broker-loss score and whether it differs from prior urgency due to changed broker data or threshold. Distinguish this judgment from rule-based concentration trim estimates. A list of headlines or chart statistics alone is not an adequate note. Do not store search snippets as full-text reviews or invent findings or citation details. Append a new note per review; preserve prior research history.

## Expanded sell-case lenses and investor questions

The examples above are not an exhaustive checklist. Select additional material lenses by issuer, business model, and evidence available: revenue quality and customer concentration; margin/price mix; cash conversion, debt maturities and refinancing; dilution and share-based compensation; capital allocation, acquisitions and integration; governance, incentives and related-party transactions; product quality, recalls, litigation and contingent liabilities; customer churn, supplier dependence, backlog/order cancellations and channel inventory; cyber, privacy, data/IP rights and technology substitution; patents, licenses, reimbursement or other business-specific dependencies; market share, industry structure, pricing power, switching costs and substitutes; working capital, pension obligations and off-balance-sheet commitments; commodity, rates, FX and regional demand sensitivity; short interest or financing only when credible evidence makes it relevant. For funds, assess mandate fit, index/holdings changes, concentration, tracking difference, fees, liquidity, structure, custody/counterparty and distribution policy. Do not force irrelevant categories into a report or imply a risk exists merely because it is on this list.

Persist the expanded review as a readable, paragraph-separated `summary`, generally 4–8 substantive paragraphs and about four times longer than a brief prior note. Cover material lenses for that issuer or fund: position/return context; fundamentals and financial quality; management/governance/execution; moat, competitors and industry economics; operational, customer/supplier, cyber, macro, legal/regulatory, geopolitical, or other material factors; balanced sell case/countercase; and indicators and unknowns. Go beyond these examples when relevant. State when a factor is not evidenced; don't force every category into every report. Include dated source citations and identify which full texts were read. Explicitly record whether reading changed the business/thesis assessment and why; separately state that urgency is the mechanical broker-loss score and explain any difference from an earlier score only by changed broker data or threshold. Keep this separate from concentration-trim estimates. A list of headlines or chart statistics alone is inadequate. Do not store snippets as full-text reviews or invent facts/citations. Append a note per review and preserve history.

End a researched digest with up to five pointed, answerable questions only when the user's answers could materially change the thesis assessment or how the user applies it. Ground each question in a documented finding or a specific unknown, explain briefly why it matters, and label it as unanswered investor context—not evidence. Useful questions may concern the original thesis/portfolio role, which measurable operating or governance development would invalidate the thesis, willingness to tolerate a documented legal/geopolitical/regulatory exposure, or whether a position is intended as a deliberately speculative allocation. The user has no fixed holding horizon, reinvests dividends, and prefers to sell when long-term prospects materially deteriorate; do not ask for a horizon as if one were required. Avoid generic "what would make you sell?" prompts and do not presume answers. If no answer is needed to complete the factual analysis, do not ask merely to ask.

## Limits

- The default broker-reported cost-basis loss trigger is 15% and is configurable; it is a review trigger, not a universal risk limit.
- The separate market-price trigger is 20% below the highest completed daily close in the trailing 12 months. A minimum 350-day chart coverage is required; shorter history is insufficient to evaluate this rule.
- Urgency score is 0 below threshold; 1 at threshold; each additional 10 percentage points of loss adds one, capped at 5. It measures loss severity only and does not determine whether to sell.
- Use broker-reported unrealized P/L and cost basis; do not treat a price decline alone as a sell signal.
- Broker statement P/L may exclude dividends and may use different reporting dates across accounts. Call this out, especially for income holdings such as AT&T.
- Positions lacking cost basis or unrealized P/L are listed as not assessed, not treated as having zero loss.
- The dashboard's position-value filter is an attention aid based on latest EUR-marked position value, not a loss-risk threshold. A low-value speculative position can still lose all its value; a higher value is not proof it should be sold.
- Historical snapshot dates show only when a position first appears in available statements; they do not establish its actual purchase date or time held. The user has no fixed holding period.
- External headlines and price metadata are best-effort, may be stale or incomplete, and must be verified against primary company/regulatory sources. Dividend yield is historical/provider-reported and dividends may change or stop.
- Never state that the 15% risk budget is within or exceeded unless the user has explicitly selected and saved the risky holdings in the dashboard. Only saved classifications count; a current unsaved widget selection does not.
- No orders are placed. Database changes are limited to appending the sourced research notes described above; do not alter position, classification, limit, or historical research data.
