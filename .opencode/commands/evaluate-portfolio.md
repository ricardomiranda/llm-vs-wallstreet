# Evaluate portfolio

Run a complete portfolio review by invoking the existing commands in order: `/update-positions`, `/update-prices`, `/review-losses`, then `/recommend-positions`. Execute each command workflow before moving to the next so every analysis uses the freshest imported positions and stored prices. Do not merely print the command names, skip a blocked step, or imply later steps used fresh data when an earlier update failed.

## Workflow

1. Read `AGENTS.md`, check the working tree, and verify `output/portfolio.sqlite3` exists. Never stage or commit that database. If it is missing or has no imported snapshots, stop and ask the user to run `/update-positions`.
2. Invoke `/update-positions`. This checks for new supported statements and imports valid new files. Preserve historical snapshots and source files. If nothing new is present, proceed using existing snapshots. If statements are malformed or unsupported, report the problem; do not claim they were imported. If the command cannot establish that the database contains usable snapshots, stop.

3. Invoke `/update-prices`. Summarize latest price dates and source-price coverage. Identify unmapped or unfetched securities. Verify SQLite integrity and inspect `current_portfolio_value_eur` and `current_security_values_eur`. If the database is missing, unreadable, or integrity fails, stop further analysis and report the blocker.

4. Invoke `/review-losses` at its default 15% broker-reported cost-basis loss threshold. Explain the 0–5 severity score, broker P/L and cost basis, currency, statement date, and first-seen snapshot date for every loss position. Report positions missing valid cost basis or P/L as not assessed. First-seen is not a purchase date; broker P/L may exclude dividends. If the user supplies a different cost-basis threshold after `/evaluate-portfolio`, pass it with `--threshold-percent` instead of the default.

    Separately apply a **20% trailing stop-loss review trigger**, defined as the latest completed daily close at least 20% below the highest completed daily close in the trailing 12 months. This is separate from the broker cost-basis rule and does not change broker-loss urgency. Require at least 350 days of chart coverage; report the peak/date, latest close/date, drawdown, and coverage, or say the check is insufficient. Do not label the rolling peak an all-time or since-purchase peak. Either trigger prompts review, not an automatic sale.

   The dashboard loss-review table has a minimum current EUR position-value filter, defaulting to positions above €70. This is only an attention filter; do not imply smaller speculative positions are protected from total loss or that larger positions should automatically be sold.

5. For every position flagged at or above the cost-basis threshold, use the stored Yahoo Finance mapping to fetch on-demand chart and issuer-news context via the project’s `security_research.fetch_market_context` function, following the report behavior in `scripts/dashboard.py`. Include latest chart date and price, source currency, 52-week range, approximate one-year price-only return versus SPY when available, drawdown from the yearly high, and provider dividend estimates when returned. Also report the distinct 12-month rolling peak review rule: peak close/date, latest completed close/date, percentage drawdown, coverage dates, and triggered/not-triggered/insufficient status. Only determine status when coverage spans at least 350 calendar days. Include issuer-related dated headlines from the last 120 days, emphasizing relevant earnings, guidance, M&A, regulatory, or legal items when returned. State when no headlines or metadata are available, distinguish price return from total return, and verify material event claims using primary company or regulatory sources where possible.

    Write a useful, balanced issuer report rather than a chart-and-headline digest. For each security with saved research, make the digest substantially more comprehensive than a brief note—generally 4–8 substantive, readable paragraphs, approximately four times the length of a short prior digest. Add evidence and analysis, not padding or repetitive metrics. The checklist is deliberately open-ended: investigate any relevant issuer-specific or fund-specific driver, including (but not limited to) revenue/customer concentration and retention, unit economics, cash conversion, debt/refinancing, dilution, governance/incentives, acquisition integration, product quality/recalls, supplier and channel dependence, cyber/privacy/IP exposure, technology substitution, working capital/pensions, industry structure, pricing power, macro/FX/rates/commodity sensitivities, or fund/index structure and fees. Use only relevant lenses supported by evidence; do not pad with generic risks. Separate: (a) **Sourced facts**; (b) **Market and sector context**; (c) **Management, governance, and execution**; (d) **Business quality and competitive durability**; (e) **Operational, financial, legal, regulatory, geopolitical, and other material exposures**, distinguishing documented current events, prospective risks, and unverified claims; (f) **Evidence-based sell case and countercase**; and (g) **Unknowns and limitations**. Explicitly state when no issuer-specific constraint or causal link to the share decline was found. Explain why facts matter without converting them into certainty or inventing a reason for a price move. If primary sources are inaccessible, label secondary-source claims as such and state that independent confirmation is incomplete. If evidence is limited, say so rather than filling gaps with generic market commentary.

   For urgency 4/5 or 5/5, read at least three full, relevant articles or primary-source publications before drawing an evidence-based conclusion about possible causes or thesis changes, except when the current position is explicitly saved as the user's risk investment or its latest stored EUR value is below €10 (exactly €10 is not exempt). Determine value from the latest stored EUR position value. Prefer primary issuer/regulatory sources plus independent reputable reporting; do not count snippets, duplicate syndications, or price-move commentary as separate confirmations. Cite each source with publisher and date and summarize what it independently establishes. If three suitable full sources cannot be accessed, disclose the number read and that confirmation remains incomplete; do not imply urgency is a sell signal.

    Present sourced facts separately from analysis and unknowns. For every researched security, explicitly state whether full-article/primary-source reading changed, strengthened, weakened, or left the **business/thesis assessment** unchanged, and explain why with evidence. Separately state that article reading does **not** change the formula-based 0–5 **broker-loss urgency**; report the current score and explain any difference from a previously saved score only by changed broker P/L/cost basis or threshold. Keep the 15% broker cost-basis trigger separate from the 20% trailing-12-month peak trigger. Do not describe any factor as a cause of the share decline unless a source establishes that connection. State when the original investment thesis was not supplied; do not presume it. The user has no fixed holding period, reinvests dividends, and is willing to sell when long-term prospects materially deteriorate; do not frame a one-year holding period as their preference. End with up to five sharp follow-up questions only when answers could materially alter the assessment or personalize it to the user's circumstances. Tie each question to a specific research finding or missing context, say why it matters, and make clear it is unanswered investor context rather than evidence. Do not ask generic exit questions. Headline categories are keyword-based and search results incomplete; never claim full event coverage or treat ambiguous headlines as proof. Treat both loss and peak-drawdown triggers as review prompts, not sell rules.

6. Invoke `/recommend-positions` only after `/update-prices` has completed. If it reports missing price coverage or unconverted cash, do not present partial-sell recommendations; report the coverage issue and request corrected mappings/provider data followed by `/update-prices`. For eligible candidates, report current and target weight, estimated quantity and EUR proceeds, price date, and whether the limit is the default or security-specific. These are concentration-control suggestions, not trading instructions.

   Buying is a separate, contribution-funded workflow. Do not infer buy candidates from loss flags or concentration. If the user asks for a gradual target-balancing plan, invoke `/recommend-buys` after the data refresh and sell-limit review; follow its investor-intake, role-confirmation, full-price-coverage, and source-research requirements. Do not assume a contribution amount, target-role classification, or completion horizon.

7. Only after all evaluation steps finish successfully, record the completion time in the selected portfolio database using UTC:

   ```bash
   .venv/bin/python scripts/record_evaluation.py
   ```

   This creates or updates the database's singleton evaluation timestamp. If the dashboard is configured to use a non-default database, pass it with `--database <path>`. Never update the timestamp for a partial run or a run stopped by an error.

8. State the portfolio's priced value and data freshness, then clearly separate:
     - 15% broker-reported cost-basis loss review flags and separate 20% trailing-12-month peak drawdown checks;
      - price/news context and analysis;
      - concentration trim estimates;
      - missing or stale information;
      - newly recorded notes and prior saved research history for each researched security. Save a comprehensive, paragraph-separated digest—generally 4–8 substantive paragraphs and about four times the length of a brief prior digest. Explain the sourced possible sell case and countercase using relevant issuer-specific lenses, including management/governance, financial/business quality, competition, operations, macro/sector context, and material legal/regulatory/geopolitical factors. Include the 20%-from-observed-peak trigger separately from broker P/L/urgency. End with observable indicators, limitations, whether reading changed the business/thesis assessment and why, and a separate statement that reading does not change broker-loss urgency. Add up to five evidence-specific follow-up questions only when answers could materially change or personalize the assessment. Do not pad or repeat metrics; a list of headlines or chart statistics alone is not an adequate note.

    Do not classify a position as uncertain/risky based on concentration or loss. The user's provisional role targets for these securities are 55% broad diversified market funds, 25% dependable dividend-growth companies, 15% other individual growth companies, and 5% higher-uncertainty/special situations. These are planning targets, not saved risk classifications or trade instructions. The user reinvests dividends and seeks sustainable, growing regular payouts rather than high yield alone. Classify Plus500 as individual growth/financial-trading exposure unless evidence supports durable, covered dividend growth; do not silently reclassify holdings or assert target achievement without defensible classifications and complete coverage. The 15% uncertainty exposure budget counts only the user's explicit saved classifications; if information is unavailable, do not state whether it is within or over limit. No orders are placed.

## Safety and data limits

- The invoked command workflows run project scripts with `.venv/bin/python`.
- Prices are delayed daily closes, not live quotes. Show actual stored dates, including stale prices retained when a fetch fails.
- Price mapping and news search are best-effort. Do not guess ambiguous symbols or imply complete price/news/event coverage.
- Preserve both source price/currency and EUR conversion/FX date; do not remove historical price observations.
- Broker statement P/L can omit distributions and statement dates can differ by account. Consider total return, especially for dividend-oriented positions.
- Missing cost basis/P&L means not assessed, never zero loss.
- Do not modify saved risk classifications, position limits, or trade data as part of this evaluation. Database changes are limited to importing valid new source statements, appending price/FX data through existing workflows, recording evaluation completion, and appending sourced research notes.
- Do not execute trades or change source statement files.
